# AI/ML Stock-Picking and Trading Models in Indian Markets (NSE/BSE equities, Nifty/BankNifty options): evidence, regulation, costs (as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 3 Oct 2026. About 18 search/fetch calls. Wherever possible, regulatory items are dated and the dependence on secondary sources (broker blogs, press) is flagged. Several SEBI primary PDFs were not fetched directly, so press and broker summaries stand in for them.

## Q1. What do academic studies find about ML predictability of NSE/BSE stocks (cross-section, factors, momentum), and are there rigorous out-of-sample results with costs?

### Takeaway
The best Indian cross-sectional ML study found is Lalwani & Meshram (2022, Applied Economics). It uses survivorship-bias-free data from 1994 to 2019 and 35 characteristics, and finds that ML beats OLS out of sample. Many characteristics are significant (14 with t > 3), and model-sorted portfolios earn significant returns. I could not confirm net-of-cost figures from the accessible abstract. The rest of the India ML literature (LSTM, DRL on Nifty 50) is mostly lower-tier price-forecasting work with weak cost treatment. For factor work, use IIMA's investable, survivorship-corrected four-factor library.

### Cited Findings
- Lalwani, V. & Meshram, V.V. (2022), "The cross-section of Indian stock returns: evidence using machine learning," *Applied Economics* 54(16):1814–1828, DOI 10.1080/00036846.2021.1982132. They test 35 stock characteristics on a "comprehensive, survivorship bias free sample" of all firms listed on the major Indian exchanges from 1994 to 2019. In Fama-MacBeth regressions, 14 predictors have t-stat > 3. Rolling one-month-ahead out-of-sample ML forecasts show "substantial improvement in forecast accuracy" over OLS, and investment strategies built on the forecasts earn significant returns. The abstract page does not report OOS R², long-short magnitudes, the model list, or transaction costs. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/applec/v54y2022i16p1814-1828.html)
- Broader EM evidence: ML return forecasts give superior out-of-sample returns in emerging markets. This is a secondary summary of an EM study, not India-specific. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=43678)
- The IIMA "Indian Fama-French and Momentum" data library (Agarwalla, Jacob, Varma) publishes regularly updated FF3 + momentum factor returns for India built from CMIE Prowess. Its design choices: more firms than earlier studies, exclusion of illiquid firms so portfolios are investable, India-appropriate small/big cutoffs, and correction for survival bias. The new series covers BSE + NSE. Legacy releases end in 2019 and were BSE-only. Methodology: Working Paper W.P. 2013-09-05, "Four factor model in Indian equities market." The site also has a factor drawdown page. — [IIMA data library](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/~iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/); [legacy page](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/legacy/); [WP PDF](https://web.iima.ac.in/~iffm/legacy/four-factors-India-90s-onwards-IIM-WP-Version-original-Sep13.pdf); [Jayanth Varma page](https://www.jrvarma.in/fama-french.html)
- Christ University (2024): stacked LSTM forecasts on NIFTY stocks rank names by predicted return and form 30 portfolios with six objectives. Separately, a DRL + meta-learning "signal bands" Nifty-50 portfolio paper includes transaction-cost and allocation constraints. These are institutional-repository papers, not top journals. — [Christ Univ. archive (DRL)](https://archives.christuniversity.in/items/show/25570); [search summary of LSTM study](https://archives.christuniversity.in/items/show/26744)
- A 2026 study of AI-based trading on the NIFTY 50 notes that a model can reach low forecast error without economically useful performance once transaction costs, turnover and drawdowns are counted. (From a search snippet only; the full paper was not fetched.) — [search result, IRE Journals](https://www.irejournals.com/formatedpaper/1723436.pdf)
- A Dec 2025 conference paper, "Beating the Clock: Volatility-Guided Deep Learning for Intraday Index Trading," applies a Deep Q-Network to Nifty-50 intraday trading. Only the snippet was seen; no cost-adjusted results were verified. — (search result summary; no primary URL retrieved)
- Hierarchical Risk Parity and minimum-variance portfolios on NIFTY 50 stocks (arXiv 2202.02728). This is portfolio construction, not return prediction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.02728)

### Inferences
- The cross-sectional anomaly base in India is rich: many characteristics are significant, and the market has historically been less efficient than the US. A tree/NN cross-sectional ranker on monthly data is the setup with real academic support. Daily or intraday single-stock price-forecasting papers (LSTM etc.) generally lack credible cost-adjusted out-of-sample tests and should get little weight.
- Monthly rebalancing in delivery equity pays about 0.2% STT round trip plus stamp duty (see Q4). A 100%-turnover monthly strategy then gives up about 2.5–3% a year before impact. That is survivable for a cross-sectional model whose spread is in the mid-single digits or higher. Small- and micro-cap anomalies (where Indian factor premia are often strongest) carry much larger impact and circuit-limit risks.
- Use IIMA factors as the benchmark for any ML stock picker (alpha against MKT/SMB/HML/WML), so the model does not just rediscover Indian momentum or the size premium.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve full-text OOS R², long-short Sharpe or net-of-cost results for Lalwani & Meshram. The paper is paywalled and only the abstract was accessible.
- I found no IIM/IIT working paper with a rigorous Gu-Kelly-Xiu-style replication on NSE that includes explicit Indian cost modelling (STT, stamp duty, impact). This may exist, but searches did not surface it.
- I found no reliable published estimates of the post-2020 decay of Indian momentum or factor premia. The IIMA library data could be used to compute it directly.

## Q2. What is known about ML/systematic strategies on Nifty/BankNifty options (intraday, near-close, expiry day), and what do SEBI's F&O studies and the 2024–2026 rule changes say?

### Takeaway
SEBI data shows that about 91–93% of individual F&O traders lose money. Aggregate individual losses were ₹1.8 lakh crore over FY22–24, about ₹1.06–1.12 lakh crore in FY25, and ₹91,685 crore in FY26. Meanwhile prop firms and FPIs profit, almost entirely through algorithms. Since Nov 2024 the product structure has been re-engineered: one weekly expiry per exchange, ₹15–20 lakh contract sizes, removal of the expiry-day calendar-spread benefit, upfront premium collection, and intraday delta-based position monitoring from Dec 2025. Peer-reviewed ML evidence on Nifty/BankNifty intraday options is essentially absent.

### Cited Findings
**SEBI F&O studies**
- **Sept 2024 study (FY22–FY24):** 92.8% of more than 1 crore individual traders lost money, an average of about ₹2 lakh each (including transaction costs), for a cumulative ₹1.8 lakh crore over three years. The top 3.5% of loss-makers (about 4 lakh traders) lost an average of ₹28 lakh. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/93-percentage-of-individual-traders-lost-rs18-lakh-crore-in-equity-fo-in-past-3-years-sebi/75210.html); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/93-of-1-crore-fo-traders-saw-rs-2l-avg-loss-while-top-3-5-faced-rs-28l-avg-loss-incl-costs-sebi/)
- Same study: in FY24, proprietary traders booked about ₹33,000 crore and FPIs about ₹28,000 crore in profits. 97% of FPI profits and 96% of prop profits came from algorithmic trading. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/93-percentage-of-individual-traders-lost-rs18-lakh-crore-in-equity-fo-in-past-3-years-sebi/75210.html); [Wright Research](https://wrightresearch.in/blog/sebi-futures-and-option-report-individual-traders-in-fandos-incur-rs-18-lakh-crore-loss-over-3-years)

STT paid by individuals rose to ₹27,695 crore from ₹7,893 crore. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-measures-reduce-equity-fo-losses-for-retail-investors-in-fy26); [Bajaj Broking](https://www.bajajbroking.in/share-market-news/f-and-o-losses-fall-18-percent-average-loss-per-trader-rose-to-rupees-1-16-lakh)
  - *Conflict to flag:* the FY25 figures differ between the two studies (₹1,05,603 crore vs ₹1,11,788 crore; ₹1.1 lakh vs ₹1,13,913 average). This is probably because the FY26 study used a broader broker/client universe and restated FY25. I could not confirm the FY26 "% of individuals losing" figure. The STT jump from ₹7,893 crore to ₹27,695 crore looks implausibly large for one year of rate changes alone (the April 2026 hike falls in FY27). It may reflect a different measurement basis, so verify against the SEBI PDF before relying on it.

**Rule changes (index derivatives)**
- **SEBI circular SEBI/HO/MRD/TPD-1/P/CIR/2024/132, 1 Oct 2024.** Index derivative contract value must be at least ₹15 lakh at introduction, with lot sizes set so the value on review day falls between ₹15 and 20 lakh. - The package also covered higher tail-risk coverage (ELM) on options expiry day, upfront collection of option premium from buyers, removal of calendar-spread treatment on expiry day, and intraday monitoring of position limits. — [Outlook Business FY26](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-measures-reduce-equity-fo-losses-for-retail-investors-in-fy26)

NSE weekly/monthly expiries moved to **Tuesday from Sept 2025**. BankNifty now has only monthly expiry. — [Sahi blog](https://sahi.com/blogs/nse-cuts-lot-sizes-in-nifty-bank); [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/fno-lot-size-expiry-calendar); [Finnovate example uses lot 65](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/budget-2026-stt-hike-fno-trades-explained). (Secondary sources; check NSE circulars for exact effective dates.)
- **Delta-based (FutEq) OI and intraday position monitoring.** Phased in July–Dec 2025: intraday net index-options limit of ₹5,000 crore FutEq per entity (vs ₹1,500 crore end-of-day), and gross ₹10,000 crore per side. Exchanges monitor intraday, with full enforcement and penalties/ASD after 5–6 Dec 2025 and extra penalties for breaches on expiry day. In Dec 2025 SEBI also proposed recalibrating trading-member-level limits. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-fo-revamp-new-oi-metric-position-limits-july-december-rollout-125052901798_1.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-tightens-intraday-position-limits-derivatives.html); [Probe42](https://resources.probe42.in/regulatory-updates/sebi-updates/intraday-position-limits-2025/); [NSE circular](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/SURV70553.pdf)

**Expiry-day dynamics and the Jane Street case**
- SEBI interim order, 3 Jul 2025: on 18 expiry days between Jan 2023 and Mar 2025, Jane Street entities allegedly bought BankNifty constituents and futures in the morning and sold in the afternoon to move the index in favour of large options positions ("marking the close"). SEBI ordered ₹4,843.57 crore impounded; Jane Street deposited it on 14 Jul 2025. Trading restrictions were lifted from 21 Jul 2025 on compliance. Jane Street appealed to SAT in Sept 2025, and the SAT hearing was adjourned as of 25 Feb 2026, so the matter is unresolved. — [ECGI blog](https://www.ecgi.global/publications/blog/expiry-day-and-the-governance-of-algorithmic-trading-the-jane-street-episode); [Oxford Business Law Blog](https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2025/07/jane-street-and-expiry-day-trap-unpacking-sebis-crackdown-algorithmic); [Kotak](https://www.kotaksecurities.com/news/market-news/sebi-bars-jane-street)
- Academic: an ARMA-EGARCH study of BankNifty daily closes (Jan 2010–Mar 2020) found no expiration-day effect on mean returns or volatility, and no post-expiry price reversal. — [KoreaScience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202032462596875.do)
- Practitioner (weak evidence): one analysis of 107 BankNifty expiry days found average expiry-day intraday return of about 0.03%. Only 19 of 107 days (17%) moved more than 1%. — [5paisa blog](https://5paisa.com/blog/f-and-o-expiry-day-trading-strategy-january-27); [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/option-trading-strategy-based-on-bank-nifty-last-30-mins-movement/78256)

### Inferences
- The SEBI data is the base rate for any retail options model. The edge in Indian index options has accrued to algorithmic prop firms and FPIs with execution, inventory and scale advantages, which is effectively the other side of retail flow. A retail ML model buying near-close CE/PE options is, by default, structurally in the losing population. It needs an out-of-sample edge larger than about 1% of premium per round trip after costs (see Q4) plus spread.
- Daily-close data shows no expiration effect, but the Jane Street order shows intraday expiry-day index paths could be moved by a large player. Backtests of expiry-day or near-close option strategies built on 2023–early-2025 BankNifty data may embed a regime that no longer exists (BankNifty weeklies discontinued Nov 2024; enforcement after July 2025). Treat pre-Nov-2024 weekly-option backtests as non-stationary.
- Fewer expiries (one weekly per exchange, NSE on Tuesday) and bigger lots mean fewer independent trading events per year and coarser position sizing. Both widen confidence intervals on any options backtest.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed ML study of Nifty/BankNifty intraday options with out-of-sample, cost-adjusted P&L. The intraday DQN paper (Dec 2025) was seen only as a snippet.
- I found no published study specifically on 14:30–15:30 entry option strategies on Nifty after the 2024–25 rule changes.
- The SEBI FY26 study PDF was not fetched. The "% losing" figure and any proprietary/FPI profit breakdown for FY26 are unverified.

## Q3. SEBI's retail algorithmic trading framework (Feb 2025 circular, 2025–2026 implementation): what it means for a personal model running through a broker API like Fyers

### Takeaway
Under SEBI's 4 Feb 2025 circular, all API orders are treated as algo orders that must be tagged. The NSE implementation standards (May 2025) set a 10 orders/second (TOPS) threshold. A self-developed personal algo below that threshold does not need individual exchange registration; it gets a generic algo ID. It must still use a registered App ID, a whitelisted static IP, daily 2FA, and market-price-protection orders. The framework became mandatory for all brokers on **1 April 2026** after deadline extensions. Black-box algos *offered to others* require the provider to hold SEBI Research Analyst registration.

### Cited Findings
- SEBI Circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/0000013, 4 Feb 2025, "Safer participation of retail investors in Algorithmic trading." Brokers are principals, and algo providers act as their agents and must be empanelled with exchanges. Exchanges set empanelment criteria and run surveillance and audits. The original effective date was 1 Aug 2025. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-circular-safer-participation-retail-investors-algorithmic-trading.html); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com:443/india/commoditiesderivativesstock-exchanges/1581380/sebi-circular-safeguarding-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading); [QuantInsti summary](https://blog.quantinsti.com/sebi-algo-trading-guidelines-retail-investors)
- Algos must be approved by exchanges before deployment, and exchanges get a post-trade "kill switch." There are two categories: white box (logic disclosed and replicable) and black box (logic not disclosed). Black-box providers must register as SEBI Research Analysts, keep research reports, and re-register on logic changes. — [Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/sebi-has-something-to-say-about-algo); [Liquide](https://blog.liquide.life/sebi-algo-trading-regulations-2026/); [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/corporate/sebis-new-regulatory-framework-for-retail-algorithmic-trading/)
- NSE implementation standards (May 2025; further NSE guidelines Aug 2025) set a Threshold Order Per Second (TOPS) of 10 OPS per exchange. Orders below it need no individual algo registration but are tagged as algo with a generic ID. A tech-savvy retail investor's self-developed algo must be registered (via the broker) only if it sends more than 10 orders/second. Static IPs can be shared only within "family" (self, spouse, dependent children/parents) with 2FA-verified consent. — [Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/general/a-comprehensive-overview-of-nses-circular-on-the-new-retail-algo-trading-framework); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/nse-issues-fresh-guidelines-on-retail-participation-algo-trading); [Angel One Aug 2025](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/nse-mandates-retail-algo-strategy-registration-in-new-august-2025-guidelines); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/nse-lays-down-new-ground-rules-for-retail-algo-trading-to-boost-safety-and-compliance-125050600690_1.html)
- Timeline: SEBI extended implementation (circular around 30 Sept 2025). Ready brokers could go live from 1 Oct 2025. Brokers had to register at least one retail algo product and strategy by 31 Oct 2025 and complete API-strategy registrations by 30 Nov 2025, with a mock session by 3 Jan 2026. The framework applies to **all brokers from 1 April 2026**. — [SEBI PDF (Sep 2025)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2025/1759232056254.pdf); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-extends-deadline-to-implement-retail-algo-trading-by-april-2026); [Bajaj Broking](https://www.bajajbroking.in/share-market-news/sebi-extends-algo-trading-implementation-timeline)
- **Fyers-specific notice:** the framework is effective 1 Apr 2026, with old and new running in parallel until 31 Mar 2026. Orders are accepted only from a registered App ID mapped to a whitelisted static IP. The cap is 10 orders/second. Users must complete 2FA once every trading day, and continuous refresh-token sessions are no longer supported. Market orders are auto-converted to Market Price Protection (MPP) orders. Third-party platforms must be empanelled and hosted inside the broker's infrastructure. Fyers "Automate" is offered as a compliant in-house option. — [Fyers notice board](https://fyers.in/notice-board/new-sebi-framework-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026/)
- Every algo order carries an exchange-provided strategy/algo ID, so regulators can trace anomalies to specific code. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-extends-deadline-to-implement-retail-algo-trading-by-april-2026)

### Inferences
- A personal ML model that places a handful of option orders a day through the Fyers API (well under 10 OPS) falls in the "self-developed, below-TOPS" bucket. It needs no RA licence and no individual strategy registration. The binding operational requirements are a **static IP** (a home dynamic IP or a cloud VM without a reserved IP will fail), **daily manual 2FA login** (unattended token refresh is gone, so a fully headless 14:45 job needs a morning login step), and **MPP instead of pure market orders** (fills may be rejected or partial in fast moves near close).
- If the model's signals were sold or shared with non-family users, it would become an "algo provider" product. It would then need exchange empanelment via a broker and, if black-box (as ML models typically are), RA registration.
- Exact details (TOPS value, static-IP rules, MPP bands) are set by exchange and broker circulars and have changed several times in 2025–26. Re-check NSE and Fyers notices before go-live.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the SEBI Feb 2025 circular PDF or the NSE implementation-standards PDF directly; specifics come via broker and press summaries.
- I found no data yet on how many retail self-developed algos have registered since April 2026, or on any enforcement against non-compliant API users.
- MPP protection-band percentages for index options on Fyers were not found.

## Q4. Indian transaction costs (STT incl. Oct 2024 and Apr 2026 hikes, exchange fees, GST, stamp duty, slippage) and their effect on short-horizon ML strategies

### Takeaway
Two successive STT hikes have tripled derivatives STT since 2024. Futures are at 0.05% on the sell side (from 1 Apr 2026, up from 0.02%), and option premiums at 0.15% on the sell side (up from 0.1%). With brokerage, exchange fees, GST and stamp duty, a Nifty-options round trip now costs roughly 0.7% of premium before spread, and a Nifty-futures round trip about 0.06% of notional (about 15 index points). That is a heavy hurdle for any daily-turnover ML strategy.

### Cited Findings
- **Union Budget 2026-27 (1 Feb 2026), effective 1 Apr 2026:** STT on futures rises 0.02% → 0.05% (sell side). On options premium it rises 0.10% → 0.15% (sell side). On exercised options it rises 0.125% → 0.15% of intrinsic value. Equity delivery (0.1% each side) and intraday (0.025% sell) are unchanged. Indices fell nearly 2% on Budget day. — [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/budget-2026-stt-hike-fno-trades-explained); [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/stt-hike-on-fo-to-take-effect-from-april-1-amid-rising-options-activity); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/union-budget-2026-buyback-proceeds-to-be-taxed-as-capital-gains-stt-raised-on-futures-and-options); [Swastika](https://www.swastika.co.in/big-budget-day/stt-hiked-on-futures-options-union-budget-2026-key-points)
- The government has said there will be no review of the STT structure, and F&O volumes have risen despite the hike. — [Basis Point Insight](https://www.basispointinsight.com/Story/govt-says-no-review-of-stt-structure--f-o-volumes-rise-despite-budget-tax-hike_9c6d75f3c414.html)
- Zerodha's current charge schedule (reflects post-Apr-2026 STT):
  - **Options:** brokerage ₹20/order; STT 0.15% sell (premium) and 0.15% of intrinsic value on exercise; NSE transaction charge 0.03553% of premium; GST 18% on (brokerage + exchange + SEBI fees); SEBI ₹10/crore; stamp duty 0.003% on buy side; IPFT ₹2/lakh of premium.
  - **Futures:** brokerage 0.03% or ₹20; STT 0.05% sell; NSE 0.00183%; stamp duty 0.002% on buy side.
  - **Equity intraday:** STT 0.025% sell; NSE 0.00307%; stamp duty 0.003% on buy side.
  - **Equity delivery:** STT 0.1% each side; stamp duty 0.015% on buy side.
  - — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/)
- Worked examples (Nifty at 25,000, lot 65): futures STT per sell rises from ₹325 to ₹812.50. Selling one option lot at ₹150 premium pays STT of ₹14.63 against ₹9.75 before. — [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/budget-2026-stt-hike-fno-trades-explained)
- STT paid by individual F&O traders rose to ₹27,695 crore in FY26 from ₹7,893 crore (per the SEBI FY26 study as reported; see the caveat in Q2). — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-measures-reduce-equity-fo-losses-for-retail-investors-in-fy26)

### Inferences (my own arithmetic from the Zerodha schedule; Fyers' brokerage is also a flat ₹20/order, though its exchange/IPFT pass-through may differ slightly)
- **Nifty option round trip, 1 lot (65) at ₹150 premium** (₹9,750 premium per side):
  - brokerage ₹40
  - STT ₹14.63
  - NSE transaction charge ≈ ₹6.93
  - GST ≈ ₹8.45
  - stamp duty ≈ ₹0.29
  - IPFT ≈ ₹0.39
  - SEBI fee ≈ ₹0.02
  - **Total ≈ ₹71, about 0.73% of premium, or about ₹1.1 of premium per unit.** Bid-ask slippage comes on top. At lower premiums (₹50 OTM), the fixed ₹40 brokerage pushes this above 1.5%.
- **Nifty futures round trip, 1 lot** (₹16.25 lakh notional): STT ₹812.50, plus exchange ≈ ₹59, stamp duty ≈ ₹33, brokerage ₹40, GST ≈ ₹19 and SEBI fee ≈ ₹3. **Total ≈ ₹966, about 0.06% of notional, or about 15 Nifty points.** A strategy turning over daily (about 250 round trips a year) gives up about 15% of notional a year to costs, before slippage.
- **Equity intraday round trip:** about 0.035–0.05% plus brokerage and spread. **Delivery round trip:** about 0.22%.
- Short-horizon ML strategies therefore need per-trade gross edges of several basis points (futures) or about 1%+ of premium (options) just to break even. These are the horizons where Indian ML price-forecasting papers report accuracy gains without cost modelling. Monthly cross-sectional equity selection is the regime where the cost drag (about 2–3% a year at 100% monthly turnover) is most plausibly beaten.
- The STT asymmetry matters for strategy design. Option *buyers* pay STT only if they sell (0.15% of premium) or on exercise (0.15% of intrinsic value). Holding an ITM long option to expiry triggers STT on full intrinsic value, which can be much larger than the premium-based STT. Close ITM longs before expiry.

### Gaps
- I did not re-verify the pre-Oct-2024 STT rates (options 0.0625%, futures 0.0125%, raised to 0.1% / 0.02% from 1 Oct 2024 via the July 2024 Budget) with a source in this session. The rates are widely reported but not cited here.
- I found no published, current estimates of Nifty/BankNifty option bid-ask spreads or impact costs by moneyness and time of day (e.g., at 14:45 or in the last 30 minutes). These would need to be measured from Fyers or NSE tick data.
- The Fyers-specific charge schedule was not fetched.

## Q5. Indian data sources and tools for building such models

### Takeaway
A workable stack: NSE bhavcopy and archives for EOD equity and F&O prices, broker APIs (Fyers, Kite) for intraday and options-chain data and execution, CMIE Prowess (paid) for survivorship-free fundamentals, and the free IIMA library for the FF + momentum factors.

### Cited Findings
- IIMA Indian Fama-French and Momentum factors: free, regularly updated, built from CMIE Prowess, investable (illiquid firms excluded), survival-bias corrected, and covering BSE + NSE in the new series. — [IIMA](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/~iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/)
- CMIE Prowess underlies the IIMA factors. Lalwani & Meshram used a survivorship-bias-free dataset of all listed firms from 1994 to 2019. The database is not named in the abstract, though Prowess is the typical source. — [IIMA](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/~iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/applec/v54y2022i16p1814-1828.html)
- NSE archives publish circulars (e.g., surveillance circular SURV70553 on position limits), alongside EOD bhavcopy and F&O data. — [NSE archives](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/SURV70553.pdf)
- Broker APIs (Fyers API v3, Zerodha Kite Connect) are the main retail route for intraday and option-chain data and order placement, now subject to the static-IP and daily-2FA rules (Q3). — [Fyers notice](https://fyers.in/notice-board/new-sebi-framework-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026/)
- Open-source tooling: a vectorbt-based backtesting framework is aimed at Indian markets (Marketcalls). — [vectorbt.marketcalls.in](https://vectorbt.marketcalls.in/)

### Inferences
- Historical intraday data for *expired* option contracts is a known weak point of retail broker APIs (Fyers can reject expired-symbol history). Building an options ML dataset therefore usually means archiving snapshots going forward, or buying vendor tick data (TrueData, GDFL, etc.; not verified here).
- Survivorship bias is the main trap in Indian cross-sectional ML: delistings and suspensions are frequent among small caps. Free sources (bhavcopy, current Nifty 500 constituents) are not survivorship-free unless historical index constituents and delisted stocks are reconstructed.

### Gaps
- I did not verify current pricing or access terms for CMIE Prowess, Capitaline, ACE Equity, or NSE's paid historical tick-data products.
- I did not verify whether NSE publishes historical Nifty 50/500 constituent lists in a downloadable point-in-time form.
- No India-specific open alternative-data sets (news sentiment, filings NLP) were researched.
